# Actual-footage editorial comparison — before implementing v4

## Method and limits

Both actual files were inspected, not just their briefs: uploaded **Endra MEP Demo.mp4** (174.101 s, 1920×1080, 60 fps) and **baya-full-animatic-v3.mp4** (120.000 s, 1920×1080, 24 fps). First pass sampled both at exact four-second frame intervals across their full duration. A second pass examined 58 additional Endra frames around chapter, prompt, report, export and closing transitions; Baya's rendered handoffs were inspected at their existing timestamps. Automatic scene-change candidates were cross-checked visually; that detector alone misses many dark-screen edits.

The audio streams were decoded and measured at 50 ms windows for RMS and spectral-flux onset candidates. **Audio listening is not supported by this runtime** (the attempted audio-input review was unavailable), so this report does not claim an audition of music, timbre, voice or subjective mix quality. Visual timing is directly inspected; audio timing observations below are measurements, not a claim to have heard individual effects. The supplied Endra reference remains outside the production repository. No reference frames, sound, graphics, UI, iconography, colors or animation assets are imported into Baya.

Times below identify observed intervals; most editorial boundaries are approximate to within the sampling interval, not claims of frame-exact Endra cuts. Specific detector timestamps are identified as such.

## Why v3 feels older

V3's transitions repeatedly ask the viewer to follow position, scale, opacity and a new message at once. The action is often **the presentation rearranging itself**, rather than the product responding. Reintroducing the plan also resets the viewer's mental context: source preview, standalone illumination, then a different Studio frame. Removing the cards at download makes a rich package appear to empty out. The decorative ending adds a new task—watch a line complete—after the story already ended.

The more useful lesson from Endra is **information-driven framing**, not a ban on all movement or a claim that every transition is a hard cut. Its prompt, report detail, collaboration marker and export control get the frame when they become important. Wide shots restore context. Baya can adopt that discipline while keeping its own warmer material palette, more architectural opening, conversational interfaces and review-package story.

## Audio measurements and v4 response

On decoded mono samples, Endra's overall RMS is approximately −13.9 dBFS versus Baya v3's −38.6 dBFS; source gain differs, so these are not perceptual loudness/LUFS ratings and are not a target to copy. The large difference confirms Baya's current score is very quiet in the actual encoded file, not merely in its source settings. Spectral rises occur near Endra's observed prompt/export actions (around 00:31, 00:42, 00:52, 01:04 and 02:44); this establishes measurable temporal activity, not the identity of the sounds.

V4 uses an independently authored score/cue track. Cut and confirmation times are declared in Baya's own timeline; short cues sit on New Project, sends, the Studio reveal and download completion. The bed remains continuous across cuts. Target normalization is checked on the rendered audio instead of assuming that nonzero AAC means an adequate mix. No Endra audio is sampled, transcribed into a melody, remixed or copied. Subjective listening remains an explicitly unperformed check in this runtime.

## Locked editorial direction for implementation

120 seconds. Opening 00–12; Works chapter 12–16; preserved customer clock 16–57 with selected fixed crops; Studio chapter 57–62; preserved engineering work retimed for readability 62–92; delivery chapter 92–94; retained package/progress 94–112 with detail crops; simple end card 112–120. Direct cuts between these units. Camera motion is retained only inside the opening engineering shot; edit-driven crops have constant scale and position during their shot.
